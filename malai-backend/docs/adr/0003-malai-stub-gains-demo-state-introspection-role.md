# `malai-stub` gains a demo-state-introspection role alongside its outside-world-impersonator role

The malai-webapp-v1 **Sandbox** surface (webapp ADR-0016, ADR-0017) needs two things from the malai backend: (1) read the catalogue of fictional **Counterparties** the stub already simulates, and (2) trigger simulated inbound payments against the active **AccountHolder**'s **Accounts**. Both routes are exposed by a new `malai-services/sandbox-service/` bounded context behind `/api/v1/sandbox/*`. To serve them, the service has to talk to `malai-stub` over HTTP — a direction the architecture has not used until now.

The accepted shape: introduce `malai-clients/malai-stub-client` and `malai-clients/malai-stub-client-test-support` as a normal client pair, depended on by `sandbox-service` only. `malai-api` aggregates `sandbox-service` exactly the way it aggregates every other service module — it has no direct dependency on `malai-stub-client`. The stub gains a new `GET /demo/counterparties` route that returns its `StubFictionalDirectory` rows in the same wire shape the Sandbox UI consumes; the existing `POST /demo/inbound/{rail}` routes are reused unchanged for the simulator.

## Considered options

- **`malai-stub-client` consumed by `sandbox-service`, stub exposes a new GET route** *(accepted)*. Reuses the established client-pair convention from CLAUDE.md (every internal HTTP call goes through a `*-client` + `*-client-test-support` pair). The new direction (`malai-api → stub` over HTTP) is the smallest new surface area that delivers the brief and stays inside the existing patterns. `sandbox-service`'s controllers are thin pass-throughs / idempotency-enforcers; the stub remains the single source of truth for the directory.
- **Duplicate the fictional directory into `account-service`'s bank directory, keep the stub one-way (outbound only).** Would preserve the "stub impersonates the outside world; nothing inside malai talks to it" story. Rejected: the directory is *demo* data, not real bank-directory data — it lives in the stub because the stub *is* the simulated outside world, and copying it into `account-service` smears that responsibility. The CoP path (`account-service` → stub `/cop/lookup`) already established the stub as the authoritative source of fictional-directory truth.
- **Webapp calls the stub directly, bypassing `malai-api`.** Eliminates the new direction entirely. Rejected: cookie-based auth is owned by `malai-api`; the stub has no Cognito wiring, no `MalAccountHolderIdHeader` enforcement, no idempotency layer. Bypassing `malai-api` would force the stub to grow all of that, which is the opposite of its purpose.
- **Add the Sandbox controllers to `payments-service` (inbound) and `account-service` (counterparties), no new service module.** Rejected: the Sandbox is its own bounded context — the controllable demo-state surface — and shoe-horning it across two existing services confuses both. The new-module cost is documented and small (CLAUDE.md §"Adding a new API").

## Consequences

- **`malai-stub` now plays two roles**: its original **outside-world impersonator** role (SQS-out via gateways, scheme submit/ack/settle/return surface, CoP lookup) plus a new **demo-state introspector** role (HTTP-in for catalogue reads). The `CONTEXT-MAP.md` entry for `malai-stub` is amended to acknowledge this duality. The stub is still deployed as a separate ECS service alongside `malai-api`.
- **New module pair** in `malai-clients/`: `malai-stub-client` (extends `AbstractMalaiClient`, configured from `malai.stub.base-url`) and `malai-stub-client-test-support` (WireMock stubs + response builders). Registered in the root `pom.xml`'s `<modules>` and `<dependencyManagement>`.
- **New service module** `malai-services/sandbox-service/` owns `/api/v1/sandbox/*`. Depends on `malai-stub-client` for outbound calls. `malai-api`'s POM adds `sandbox-service` to its dependency list and imports its `@Configuration` — `malai-stub-client` is reached transitively through `sandbox-service`, not declared on `malai-api` directly.
- **One new stub route**: `GET /demo/counterparties` returning the `StubFictionalDirectory` rows in the agreed wire shape — `{ sortCode, accountNumber, bank, nameOnRecord, outboundOutcome, returnReason? }`. The existing `POST /demo/inbound/{rail}` is reused for the inbound simulator; the existing `POST /demo/arm-return` and `GET /demo/fps/returns` are unchanged and outside the Sandbox path.
- **`StubFictionalDirectoryProperties` widens**: `StubAccount` gains an `outboundOutcome: OutboundOutcome` enum (`SETTLES | RETURNS | STUCK_PENDING`); `returnReason` is non-null iff `outboundOutcome = RETURNS`, with boot-time validation. The stub's FPS and BACS scheme handlers consult `outboundOutcome` per submission. Destinations not present in the directory continue to settle.
- **No new Flyway migrations.** The Sandbox catalogue is static + global (YAML-configured on the stub); per-user state (saved Payees, created Transactions) flows through the existing tables on `account-service` / `payee-service` / `ledger-service`.
- **`/api/v1/sandbox/counterparties`** is auth-required but does *not* carry `MalAccountHolderIdHeader` — the catalogue is reference data. **`/api/v1/sandbox/inbound-payments`** is auth-required, carries the holder header, and requires `Idempotency-Key` at the `malai-api` boundary; the stub call itself is not gated by idempotency (the dedup happens upstream).
- The new direction is **the first inbound-to-stub HTTP call** in the architecture. A maintainer reading `CONTEXT-MAP.md`'s `malai-stub` row should not be surprised — the row is amended to spell out the dual role.

# Cards are their own bounded context; card authorisations are not Payments; the card-gateway's authorisation leg is synchronous

The cards brief adds debit and credit cards with simulated merchant spend. We create a single `card-service` bounded context owning the **Card** entity, the issuer-side **Card authorisation** lifecycle (approve/decline → capture/reversal) for both card variants, and the credit-card product mechanics (credit limit, statement). Card spending does **not** flow through `payments-service`: a card authorisation drives the ledger's **Hold**/**Posting** primitives directly, with its own state machine. The scheme integration follows the per-rail Gateway pattern (`card-gateway` module, `malai-stub` impersonating the card scheme + merchants), with one deliberate deviation: the **authorisation leg is synchronous HTTP end-to-end** — stub → gateway → internal call to `card-service` → approve/decline in the HTTP response — while later legs (presentment/capture, reversals, refunds) arrive asynchronously via SQS `event-*` queues like every other rail. The credit card's money position is a new `account-service` `AccountType.CREDIT_CARD`, ledger-backed like every account.

## Considered options

- **`card-service` owns its own authorisation lifecycle** *(accepted)*. A card purchase is a merchant-initiated pull the bank *authorises*, then settles later — possibly for a different amount. Forcing that onto the `payments-service` **Payment** (`PENDING → SETTLED/RETURNED`, one amount, push-via-rail) would contort its glossary and bloat every `payments` row with card-only fields (merchant, MCC, auth code, card id). Real banks separate issuer processing from payment origination for the same reason. The ledger primitives (Hold, Posting) are shared; the state machine above them is not.
- **New `CARD` rail in `payments-service`.** Superficially neat (`PENDING` = hold, `SETTLED` = captured) until capture-for-a-different-amount breaks the Payment's single-amount identity. Rejected.
- **Split `card-service` / `credit-card-service`.** Mirrors real bank org charts, but debit and credit authorisations differ only in which account the hold lands on; two services means cross-service calls on every auth at demo scale. The seam can still be drawn later if the credit product grows interest/arrears. Rejected for v1.
- **Synchronous authorisation leg** *(accepted)* vs. fully-async SQS. Real issuer links are synchronous (ISO 8583 request/response while the customer stands at the till), and the demo needs the purchase to appear instantly. Fully-async would be pattern-pure but misrepresents how card networks work and adds stub-side correlation for no gain. The documented "Gateways bridge SQS ↔ HTTP" rule is deliberately broken on this one leg; `card-gateway` remains dumb and stateless — it maps scheme-shaped auth requests onto an internal `card-service` call and translates the decision back, making no decisions itself.
- **Stub calls `card-service` directly (no gateway).** Erases the seam that answers "where would Mastercard/Visa plug in?" — the question the demo exists to answer. Rejected.

## Consequences

- New modules: `malai-services/card-service`, `malai-services/card-gateway`, plus the paired `malai-clients/malai-card-client` and `malai-card-client-test-support`. `malai-api` bundles both service modules; `malai-stub` gains card-scheme impersonation (merchant simulation, auth origination, presentment/reversal callbacks).
- `account-service` gains `AccountType.CREDIT_CARD`; the credit card's balance, transactions, and statement history ride the existing ledger-composition endpoints. Excluded from Confirmation of Payee and FPS addressing.
- The ledger needs a per-account floor (available balance may not drop below −creditLimit) — the funds guard stays the ledger's invariant, not the caller's.
- `CONTEXT-MAP.md` points at `card-service/CONTEXT.md`, which defines **Card**, **Card authorisation**, **Credit card account**, **Credit limit**.
